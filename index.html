Warrior Tank Guide: Tauren Horde

Protection Warrior for World of Warcraft: Forever, from level 1 to 60. Sources read on November 17, 2026. Devastate does not appear in any source. Shield Slam (level 40 talent) is the main Protection ability.

Wowhead Forever is the main source here (it has the most complete text). Mobalytics and Icy Veins blocked automated reading, so I used only their search summaries. Warcraft Tavern is beta-only (capped at level 30). Items marked Sources differ list each source's version. Items marked Not confirmed were not found in a source I could read.

1. Leveling overview

  • Warriors get a big boost at level 16, when they learn Shield Block and Revenge. Wowhead says Protection is strong for leveling and that Arms and Fury Warriors may switch to it for the first 35 to 40 levels. Confirmed by Wowhead (November 6, 2026).
  • Protection has no run-speed boost. Charge is available from level 4, Intercept from level 30. Confirmed by Wowhead.
  • Victory Rush (new in Forever): heals 10% of max health and can be used for 20 seconds after killing a non-trivial enemy. Its cooldown is 30 seconds. Confirmed by Wowhead and Icy Veins.
  • Wowhead's leveling advice: pool rage between mobs. Demoralizing Shout and Thunder Clap cut the enemy's damage by 30 to 40%, so you spend less time eating.

Talent path while leveling (Wowhead)

  • Get Shield Specialization, Improved Revenge and Master of Defense first. Wowhead calls these the most impactful talents for Protection.
  • After that, the deep Protection route is recommended for most players: Defiance (dungeons), Vanguard (keeps rage without swapping to Charge), and Shield Slam.
  • The Arms route (Sweeping Strikes, Impale, Weaponmaster) is for experienced players who want cleave and are willing to stance-dance. The Fury route (Blood Craze, Enrage, Gore Drinker) is for more sustain later on.
  • Point-by-point talent ranks for leveling are in the Kami-Labs build (see section 2). Wowhead's talent page shows its builds as calculator images, not text.

Leveling rotation (Wowhead, same as the endgame priority)

  • Single target: Shield Block (off the global cooldown) → Revenge → Shield Slam (once talented) → Bloodthirst (Fury variant only) → keep Sunder Armor at 5 stacks in groups → keep Thunder Clap, Battle Shout and Demoralizing Shout up → Rend if you're already high on threat → Sunder Armor with spare rage and globals → Heroic Strike above 30 rage (off the global cooldown).
  • AoE: Thunder Clap → Shield Block (off the global cooldown) → Revenge → Shield Slam → Bloodthirst → keep Battle Shout and Demoralizing Shout up → Cleave above 30 rage (off the global cooldown).
  • Execute is the top damage ability below 20% health, but you must leave Defensive Stance to cast it. Don't swap while rage is high or incoming damage is a concern.

Camping buffs (Wowhead)

  • Early: Sharpening Wheel (Strength), Faction Banner (Spirit), Lodestone (Attack Power).
  • Mid: Camp Tent becomes worth it around level 22 to 25; Camp Chair gives 2% crit and cannot be found elsewhere.
  • End: Camp Tent (disproportionate value in later levels), Camp Chair, and a First Aid Kit if you run dungeons without a Priest.

2. Talent builds at level 60 (Wowhead, plus Kami-Labs for levels 10 to 60)

Wowhead's builds (named, not point-counted in the text): Standard (Enrage 5, Deflection 4 and Anticipation, with Focused Rage as the key rage talent), Impale (Impale and Deep Wounds, with Rend scaling with Attack Power and Vanguard for quality of life), Fury-Prot (Flurry and Shield Slam, Bloodthirst, Death Wish for short fights), Dual Wield (no Protection points; for enemies whose melee attacks cannot be blocked, dodged or parried), and a Dungeon build (deep Protection, or a cleave build with Sweeping Strikes that requires stance-dancing).

Wowhead's standard build: Enrage uptime is about 67% against one target and nearly 100% against two or more. It skips most rage-reduction talents (Improved Thunder Clap, Improved Bloodrage, Improved Sunder Armor) because Wowhead's rage model makes them weak.

Sources differ The Kami-Labs 41 to 60 build (Protection 31, Fury 15, Arms 5) does not match Wowhead's Standard build point for point, and Mobalytics' talent grid conflicts with its own order (see section 3).

4. Armor, weapons and the level 30 weapon quest

  • Weapons: Wowhead says rage generation is now normalized, so the highest-level weapon is the priority. If a fast and a slow weapon have similar stats, pick the fast one. Thrash Blade (Maraudon quest) is the best alternative until level 60 (Wowhead). Confirmed by Wowhead.
  • Shield: take the highest armor shield you find. After level 40, Block Value also matters because it powers Shield Slam (Wowhead and Icy Veins).
  • Level 30 Whirlwind Weapon quest: Tauren start it with Torm Ragetotem in Thunder Bluff. It goes through Fray Island and Alterac Mountains, with an elite level 40 boss (Polestar). Rewards: Whirlwind Axe, Sword or Warhammer. Confirmed that all three are two-handers with item level 40, +16 Strength and +15 Stamina (ConquestCapped, LFCarry). They are DPS weapons, not tank weapons.
  • Sources differ on where Berserker Stance comes from. Warcraft Tavern says "The Affray". Polestar lists it as the end of the Whirlwind chain.

10. Level 60 endgame

  • Icy Veins Shield Slam tank: Arms 5, Fury 15, Protection 31. Named talents: Deflection, Anticipation, Shield Specialization, Master of Defense, Improved Shield Wall, Shield Slam, Cruelty, Enrage, Lingering Rage. Swaps for low rage are listed in the source.
  • Wowhead's endgame builds (Standard, Impale, Fury-Prot, Dungeon) are in section 2.
  • Sources differ on the final split across sources (Icy Veins 5/15/31; Mobalytics 3/16/32 in its talent grid).
  • Mobalytics' level 60 tab is marked "WIP". Raids open December 9, 2026.
  • Not confirmed as a single build for launch.
